\section{Introduction}\label{s:introduction}

Power operations on the cohomology of a simplicial complex were discovered by Steenrod and others around 1950. The essential ingredient in their construction is a homotopy commutative coproduct on its chain complex. For $p=2$, this homotopy commutativity is encoded in a $\Sigma_2$-equivariant map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:st2}
	\mathfrak{m}\colon \ES_2\ot \chains(X;\bF_2)\to \chains(X;\bF_2)\ot \chains(X;\bF_2)
\end{equation}
where $\ES2$ is a contractible free $\bF_2[\Sigma_2]$-chain complex and we let $\Sigma_2$ act trivially on the other factors. The coproduct in homology is recovered as $x\mapsto \mathfrak{m}(a\ot x)$, where $a$ is any cycle whose homology class generates the homology of $\ES_2$.

For $p$ an odd prime, the homotopy commutativity is encoded in a $\Sigma_p$-equivariant map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stp}
	\mathfrak{m}\colon \ES_p\ot \chains(X;\bF_p)\to \chains(X;\bF_p)^{\ot p}
\end{equation}
These maps exist for arbitrary coefficients and for all natural numbers $p$ and together have the richer structure of an operad, but we will not pursue this structure here. These maps were made explicit in \cite{mcclure2003multivariable} and \cite{berger2004combinatorial} (see also the more recent account \cite{BM}) taking as $\ES_p$ the Surjection and the Barrat--Eccles operads. 

In practice, it is more convenient to work with the cyclic group $\Cyc_p$ instead of the symmetric group, because it has smaller resolutions and the inclusion map $\Cyc_p\to \Sigma_p$ induces an epimorphism in homology. Taking as $\EC_p$ the minimal resolution $\W{p}$ (see \cref{s:unstable}) and as $\ES_p$ the Barrat-Eccles operad, in \cite{medina2021may_st} a map $\W{p}\to \ES_p$ was described, whose composition with the above maps yields
\[
	\W{p}\ot \chains(X;\bF_p)\to \chains(X;\bF_p)^{\ot p}.
\]
One of the interests of this latter map is that it allows to give explicit cochain-level formulas for the power operations on the cohomology of $X$.
 
These maps had been already considered by May \cite{May} in more generality: He defined a category whose objects were chain complexes $\uchains$ endowed with a comultiplication $m\colon \uchains\to \uchains\ot \uchains$ and structural maps $\mu\colon \W{p}\ot \uchains\to \uchains^{\ot p}$ that factor through a resolution of the symmetric group and such that $\mu(e_0\ot x) = m(x, m(x,\ldots))$ (we follow \cite{medina2021may_st} in calling these \emph{Steenrod-May structures}). He proved that for each prime $p$, the cohomology of these chain complexes has power operations that satisfy a Cartan relation with the coproduct $m$. This generalization allows to describe cohomology operations on the cohomology of other objects than simplicial complexes, such as Hopf algebras.

On the other hand, there are objects that support power operations but not a comultiplication, spectra being the main example. A chain-level description of the stable version of \eqref{eq:st2} and \eqref{eq:stp} was given in \cite{Gill}, where stable versions of the Surjection operad and the Barrat-Eccles operad $\ESst\Sigma_p$ were given. Let us mention that these are operads in unbounded chain complexes. One can define a stable version $\Wst{p}$ of $\W{p}$ and maps $\Wst{p}\to \ESst\Sigma_p$, and if $X$ is a $\Sigma$-spectrum, end up with smaller models
\[
 \Wst{p}\ot \chains(X;\bF_p)\to \chains(X;\bF_p)^{\ot p}
\]
which are obtained using that one can define the chain complex of a $\Sigma$-spectrum as a direct limit of the chain complexes of simplicial sets\federico{This construction works better for cochains, because then both $\Wst{p}$ and $\cochains(X)$ form an inverse system.}, and constructing the stable May-Steenrod structure as a limit of the unstable May-Steenrod structures.

One can then define a \emph{stable Steenrod-May structure} on a chain complex $\uchains$ as a collection of chain maps
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tatediag}
\Wst{p}\ot \uchains\to \uchains^{\ot p}
\end{equation}
that factor through $\ESst(p)$ and again, the cohomology of these chain complexes has power operations. A \emph{weak stable May--Steenrod structure} is a stable May--Steenrod structure where the collection of chain maps do not need to factor through $\ESst{p}$. Such a structure endows the cohomology of $\uchains$ with power operations of all degrees. Finally, we remark that there is a map $\Wst{p}\to \W{p}$, and thus a May--Steenrod structure induces also a stable May--Steenrod structure.

In \cref{s:stable} we notice that, if $X$ is a $\Sigma$-spectrum, the map
\[
	\chains(X)\lra \left(\chains(X)^{\ot p}\right)^{t\Cyc_p}
\] 
induced by the Tate diagonal $H\bF_p\to \left(H\bF_p^{\ot p}\right)^{t\Cyc_p}$ is the quotient of the adjoint of the map \eqref{eq:tatediag} by the action of the cyclic group.

 
 \subsection*{Purpose of the article} In this article we try to answer the following question: 
 \begin{quotation}
 	\emph{How to define a stable Steenrod-May structure on a chain complex that is not presented as the stabilization of chain complexes with a Steenrod-May structure?} 
 \end{quotation}
 The case that drove us to this question is the cochain complex of Khovanov homology, which computes the cohomology of a spectrum \cite{LS14, LLS20}, but is not obtained as the cochain complex of a $\Sigma$-spectrum of simplicial sets. When $p=2$, Lipshitz and Sarkar gave formulas to compute $\mathrm{Sq}^2$ \cite{LS14b}, and later the first author found a stable May-Steenrod structure for $p=2$ on the Khovanov chain complex \cite{CM-Steenrod}. 
 
  In this article we present an answer to this question for the chain complex of an augmented $p$-cyclic coalgebra. This setting is not general enough to cover Khovanov homology, and in a subsequent paper we will extend the construction of this paper to endow the Khovanov chain complex with a weak stable May--Steenrod structure for each odd prime $p$.
  
The chain complex of a simplicial set is covered by our theorem, and we stress that in this case, our stable May-Steenrod structure is not obtained from the May-Steenrod structures for simplicial sets previously mentioned. In particular, we do not use the Alexander-Whitney diagonal and we cannot easily witness the existence of a comultiplication. In exchange, our construction satisfies the property $\power^{0}=\Id$ by definition and makes use of the join operation between simplicial sets instead of the product. Another property of our formulas for $\power^i$ is that they have a simpler description when $i$ is smaller, while the formulas of \cite{medina2021may_st} have a simpler description when $i$ is large. 

The weak stable May--Steenrod structure on the chain complex of an augmented simplicial set has been implemented in \cite{github-oddp}, and can be compared to the May--Steenrod structure on the chain complex of a simplicial set implemented in \cite{github-comch}.
 
% In this article we give a general construction that endows the chain complex of a pointed augmented simplicial set with a weak stable May-Steenrod structure. Pointed augmented simplicial sets $X$ do not admit a natural diagonal $X\to X\times X$ (this is caused by the fact that the augmentation is outside of the support of the degeneracies), thus they are not candidates to support a May-Steenrod structure. Nonetheless, it is reasonable to expect that they admit a stable May-Steenrod structure, because the chain complex of the suspension of a pointed augmented simplicial set is the chain complex of a pointed simplicial set. 
 
 
 
 \subsection*{Main result} 
Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $p$ a prime number. A \emph{$p$-cyclic coalgebra} is an $R$-module $M$ with a diagonal $\diag_p\colon M\to M^{\ot p}$ that is equivariant with respect to the action of the cyclic group $\Cyc_p$. A \emph{morphism of $p$-cyclic coalgebras} is a homomorphism $f\colon M\to M'$ that commutes with the diagonals. The prototypical examples of these coalgebras are the free $R$-module on a set and the free $R$-module on a pointed set: If $X$ is a set, the $p$-fold diagonal map $X\to X\times \overset{p}{\ldots}\times X$ induces a homomorphism $R\langle X\rangle\to R\langle X\rangle^{\ot p}$. A less typical example are the free modules on a signed set (\cref{s:categories}). Since a coalgebra is in particular an $R$-module, a simplicial object in $p$-cyclic coalgebras $X$ has an associated normalized chain complex $\chains(X)$ \federico{What happens with the Dold--Kan correspondence for augmented simplicial abelian groups?}.

\begin{definition}
	A \emph{$p$-cyclic straightening with duality} is a $\Cyc_p$-equivariant choice of element $x_\tau\in \tau$ for every proper non-empty subset $\tau \subset \{0,1,\dots,r-1\}$ such that the predecessor of $x_\tau$ is not in $\tau$.
\end{definition}
We mention that this structure can exist only if $p$ is prime. Assume now that $p$ is an odd prime or that $p=2$ and $R=\bF_2$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
	Each $p$-cyclic straightening with duality yields an effective construction of a weak stable May-Steenrod structure on the normalized chain complex of an augmented simplicial $p$-cyclic coalgebra.
\end{theorem}

\subsection*{Sketch of the construction} Let $X$ be an augmented simplicial $p$-cyclic coalgebra (see \cref{s:preliminaries} for the degree conventions on the chain complexes):

\subsubsection*{Step 1} Let $\Wdualaug{r}$ be the dual of the augmented minimal resolution of the cyclic group of order $r$:
	\[
		R \lra R[\Cyc_r]\langle e_1^{\vee}\rangle \lra R[\Cyc_r]\langle e_2^{\vee}\rangle 
		\lra R[\Cyc_r]\langle e_3^{\vee}\rangle \lra \ldots
	\]
In \cref{s:2bdiagonals} we will construct a semi-simplicial structure $\susp{\bullet} \Wdualaug{r}$ on this chain complex with face maps of degree $r-1$, and describe a chain map
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uno}
	\Wst{r}\ot \chains(X) \lra \chains(\Wdualaug{r}\ot X_{\mathrm{nd}}),
\end{equation}
where $X_{\mathrm{nd}}$ denotes the semi-simplicial $R$-module obtained by quotienting by the degeneracies.
\subsubsection*{Step 2} There is an isomorphism between the cosimplicial chain complex $\chains(\asimplex^{\bullet})$ and its linear dual $\cochains(\asimplex^{\bullet})$ to which we refer as \emph{Alexander duality}. As a consequence, $\cochains(\asimplex^{\bullet})$ is endowed with a cosimplicial structure, and so is $\cochains(\asimplex^{\bullet})^{\ot r}$. Writing $\cochains(\asimplex^{\bullet})_{\mathrm{int}}^{\ot r}$ for the interior simplices (i.e., those that are not the image of coface maps), we explain how to endow $\cochains(\asimplex^{\bullet})_{\mathrm{int}}$ with a semi-simplicial structure with face maps of degree $r-1$. In \cref{s:3complexes} we construct an isomorphism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dos}
	 \chains(\cochains(\asimplex^{\bullet})_{\mathrm{int}}^{\ot r}\ot X_{\mathrm{nd}}^{\ot r})
	 \overset{\cong}{\lra}
	 \chains(X)^{\ot r}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{Step 3} The construction of our main theorem will be completed building for each $n$ a map of chain complexes 
\[
\Wdualaug{r}\lra \cochains(\asimplex^{n})^{\ot r}_{\mathrm{int}}.
\]
that commutes with the face maps of the semi-simplicial structure on each side. To simplify computations, we actually find the linear dual of this map:
\[
	\Psi\colon \chains(\asimplex^n)_{\mathrm{int}}^{\ot r}\lra \Waug{r}. 
\]
and check that it commutes with the coface maps of the cosemi-simplicial structure on each side. Once this is obtained, the map $\Psi^{\vee}\ot \diag_p$ gives a map from the target of \eqref{eq:uno} to the source of \eqref{eq:dos}.

We construct the map $\Psi$ by first observing that $\chains(\asimplex^n)_{\mathrm{int}}^{\ot r}$ is isomorphic to $\chains(\partial\asimplex^{r-1})^{\ot n+1}$ with a very simple cosemi-simplicial structure (\cref{s:milnor}), and then building chain maps
\[
	\chains(\partial\asimplex^{r-1})^{\ot n+1}
	\overset{\mathrm{red}}{\lra}
	\chains(\partial\simplex^{r-1})^{\ot n+1}_+
	\overset{\psi_{\mathrm{red}}}{\lra}
	\Per{r}
	\overset{\phi}{\lra} 
	\Waug{r},
\]
where the subscript $+$ denotes a natural augmentation of the non-augmented chain complex $\chains(\partial\simplex^{r-1})^{\ot \bullet}$, and the chain complex $\Per{r}$ is the augmented periodic resolution of the cyclic group $\Cyc_r$. Both complexes are endowed with cosemi-simplicial structures and the chain maps commute with them in all cases except for $\phi$, when they commute up to a constant. There will be a map $\psi_{\mathrm{red}}$ for each $p$-cyclic straightening with duality. We remark that $\phi$ could be redefined so that it commutes on the nose at the cost of having to take $R=\bF_p$-coefficients instead of arbitrary coefficients.
